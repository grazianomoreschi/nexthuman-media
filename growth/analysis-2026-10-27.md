# Why we stop at ~1,400 views and get almost no followers (analysis 2026-10-27)

Data: 27 Instagram reels at least 6 h old (stats/reels.json), account insights, profile, YouTube.

## 1. Two gates, not one
- Every reel ends in one of two places: ~110-210 views (14 reels) or 1,343-2,033 views (13 reels). Nothing in between,
  nothing above 2,033.
- Gate 1 (~130 people): Instagram's first test. Decided by skip rate: reels that pass have median skip 29% vs 44%.
  Format v2 (claim at frame 1, 20 s) fixed most of this: since 25/10 about 2 in 3 reels pass.
- Gate 2 (~1,100 reached people): the second test pool. **All 13 reels that reached it stopped there**, within 12 h.
  Reach is 99.9% non-followers (5,700 of 5,702 today), so discovery works; the pool simply doesn't react.

## 2. What the second pool did (13 breakout reels, 15,438 accounts reached)
- Likes: 152 = 1.0% of reach.
- Shares (sends): 13 = 0.08% of reach. Saves: 10 = 0.06%. Comments: ~0 (the only comment on each reel is our pinned one).
- Average watch: 8-17 s, 38-80% of a 20 s reel; nobody rewatches (watch % never above 100%).
- Mosseri (Instagram, 2025) names watch time, like rate and send rate as the three main Reels signals; sends matter most
  for reaching non-followers. Our watch time is decent; like rate and send rate are near zero. That is the wall.

## 3. Why people watch but don't react
- The viewer is passive: each reel is a fact told to them. Nothing to answer, try, guess or prove.
- No reason to tag a friend: the CTA "send this to the friend who..." names a person, but the content gives no
  challenge to send ("try this", "you'll fail this").
- No face, no voice (until 28/10), no recurring character: nothing to recognise or connect with.
- Comments: we removed question CTAs in v2; there is nothing a viewer can answer in one word.

## 4. Why nobody follows (6 followers from ~26,000 accounts reached = 0.02%)
- Profile visits: 8-11 a day from 5,700-8,900 reached (0.1-0.2%): viewers don't get curious about the account.
- No promise: every reel is a standalone random fact (bones, sea, space, sugar, psychology). A follow needs a reason:
  "what do I get tomorrow?".
- Profile mismatch: name "Next Human | Longevity & AI", bio "Your body. Your future. Explained in pixels." Content is
  body tricks, survival and psychology; no longevity/AI. Visitors can't see what they'd get.
- No series and no numbering, so there is no "I want the next one".

## 5. Not the problem (checked)
- Posting volume: per-reel reach stayed ~1,100-1,500 with 5-7 reels a day (no penalty seen).
- Time slot: breakouts happen at every slot (02, 06, 10, 16, 19, 22). No slot is clearly better with this sample.
- Length: 20 s reels already hold 38-80%.

## 6. Fix for 29/10 (5 reels a day)
1. **Interactive "BODY TEST" series**: the viewer does something with their own body (stare, close one eye, say the
   colour, count, look at your nose). Tests create rewatches (watch % above 100%), one-word comments ("RED", "I saw
   it") and sends ("try this"), the three signals we lack. Viral proof: hearing/vision tests and attention tests are
   among the most watched psychology/body shorts (e.g. Zack D. Films Milgram 43.9M, "How Old Are Your Ears? (test)"
   AsapSCIENCE 24M).
2. **Numbered series + follow promise**: title "BODY TEST #1...#5", closing "Follow: a new body test every day".
3. **Comment question answerable in one word** in the last segment and in the caption's first line.
4. **Send line as a challenge**: "Send this to a friend and see if they pass".
5. Keep v2 rules (frame 1 = the test and the instruction, timer that runs) and the v3 voice.
6. Profile (Graziano, by hand): name "Next Human | Body Tests", bio "1 body test a day 🧠 Can your body pass?
   Real science, in pixels.", pin the 3 best reels (Swabs, Ice Gym, Pill).
Measure (E4): likes per reach > 3%, shares per reach > 0.5%, comments > 5 per reel, watch % > 100%, follows per day.

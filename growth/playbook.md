# Next Human playbook (living rules)

Status: PROVEN (our data confirms it on 3+ reels per variant), TESTING (in an open experiment or applied while
data comes in), IDEA (hypothesis, not applied yet), REJECTED (our data says no). Every change of status gets
the date and the evidence. Updated by the reviews (skill `next-human-growth`).

Baseline on 2026-10-23 (4 Instagram reels, new account, 1 follower): skip 31-51% (median ~44%), watch 25-32%
of 36-44 s (avg 11-12 s), ~0 shares/saves/comments, 120-139 views each.

## Hook and first frame
1. PROVEN (2026-10-25). Frame 1 already shows the subject, the key claim and the number. No subtitle intro, no
   title-only frame. Evidence: v2 reels (claim at frame 1, no intro) skip 29.1% / 19.6% / 20.6% (Muscle, Swabs,
   Cut), all below the best of 11 v1 reels (32%, which had a 4 s intro; median 43%). All 3 left the first test
   (v1: 2 of 11). Caveat: bundled with length and moving hook (E1), so the part that works is not isolated.
   Mosseri, TikTok tips (first 3 s). Since 2026-10-23.
2. PROVEN (2026-10-24). Hook = "your" + a physical situation the viewer can feel (ice water, hour 17 awake,
   last cigarette). Never "this/these" + an abstract object (cells, a shot, "your brain right now").
   Evidence: you-situation hooks skip 32% / 33% / 38% (No Sleep, Cold Plunge, Quit Smoking), abstract hooks
   50% / 51% / 59% (Stroke, Ozempic, 80 Years Old). Median 33% vs 51%, n=3+3, all v1 format. Cold Plunge
   (best hook) left the first test: 1,400 views vs median 136. 2026-10-24 22:00 check: Space ("Astronauts come back
   from space taller", concrete surprising body fact, v1) also left the test: 1,306 views, skip 32.8%, watch 37.5%;
   Clock Shift ("your clock", not a physical situation) skip 50.9%. Rule stands; the working pattern is a concrete,
   felt or surprising body fact, not "your" alone.
   2026-10-25 23:05: Ice Water ("You fall into ice water", v2) skip 16.8%, lowest ever; Taller ("Doctors can break
   your legs", third person) skip 41.6%, worst v2. Same format, so the subject of the hook decides.
   2026-10-25 23:35 correction: Ice Water skip was a 1 h read; at 1.5 h it is 38.1% vs Taller 41.7%, so this pair
   does not support the rule. Rule stays PROVEN on the 2026-10-24 evidence.
2b. TESTING (2026-10-24). Frame 1 is a body IN ACTION (falling into ice, holding breath, climbing), not a lab
   object (cell, dish, chart, pen) and not the generic brain opener. 2026-10-26: Fluid Brain ("Your brain fills your skull")
   37 views/h at 3 h, skip 35.5%, vs Ice Gym 259/h, skip 26.4%: one more case for it.
   2026-10-26 23:05: Fluid Brain final 127 views (skip 35.7%); Spit Boil (vacuum, 182 views, skip 38.9%) and Back from Space
   (133 views, skip 44.6%) also stayed in the first test: remote subjects (space, vacuum, brain trivia) stall, while the
   body-in-a-habit reels (Ice Gym, Sleep Paralysis, After Death) left it. Evidence: Cold Plunge (plunge, sea) and
   No Sleep (drunk) vs 80 Years Old (cell, dish, tube) and Ozempic (chart, pen).
   2026-10-27 23:05: Breath (holding breath, action at second 0) 1,806 views, skip 26%, watch 69.5%; Death Zone (remote altitude) 528, skip 48.4%:
   two more cases for the rule.
2c. TESTING (2026-10-24). Aggressive, moving hook (Graziano): at 0.0 s a punch-in or shake plus a boom, the
   big number already counting, the timer already running. Nothing static in the first second.
3. IDEA. Open on the end state ("At 72 hours your body starts eating itself"), then rewind the timer to 0.

## Length and pacing
5. PROVEN (2026-10-25). 20-25 s reels (4-6 timer stops) instead of 35-45 s until watch % beats 50%. Evidence: avg watch
   11-12 s on every reel regardless of length; shortest reel (36 s) had the best watch % (32%). Since
   2026-10-23.
   2026-10-25: v2 watch 56.8% / 82.4% / 53.5% (13.3 / 17.3 / 10.7 s) vs v1 28%; seconds up only ~20% (part of
   the % gain is arithmetic), so it stays TESTING until E1 closes.
   PROVEN (2026-10-25 23:05, E1 closed): watch % beats 50% (median 55.8%, n=5 v2 vs 28.3%, n=11 v1) and 5/5 left
   the first test. Caveat: average seconds watched are flat (~11.5 s in both), so length helps the % and the exit
   rate, not the seconds; the lever to push next is the payoff before second 10.
6. TESTING. Every stop beats the previous one; re-hook ("but...") at ~40-50%. (Loewenstein 1994.)
   2026-10-24: the fact worth sending lands by second 10 (average watch is 11.5 s; even the best reel holds
   17 s), the twist at the end only adds to it. Cold Plunge: 0 shares on 1,087 reach - the payoff came too late.
7. TESTING. A change on screen every 1.5-3 s (timer tick, scene, punch-in, counter, sound hit).
8. IDEA. Loop ending: the last frame matches the first so the replay feels seamless.
